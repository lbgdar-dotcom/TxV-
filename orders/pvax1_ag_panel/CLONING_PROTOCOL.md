## How each fragment is built

```
 5'─[ 20-bp left arm ]─[ ATG ················ ORF ················ TGA TAA ]─[ 20-bp right arm ]─3'
      GAAGAAATATAAGAG                                                          GCTGCCTTCTGCGGGGCTTG
      + GCCACC (Kozak)
```

The **left arm is not padding** — it is the last 20 nt of your backbone's 5' UTR
*including the Kozak*. The fragment therefore starts at `ATG` and must not carry
its own Kozak. The right arm is the first 20 nt of the retained 3' UTR. In the
assembled plasmid each arm appears exactly once, because homology arms are
consumed as overlap, not added.

Both arms are unique within every fragment, so there is one unambiguous way for
each to assemble.

## Cloning

**1. Linearise the backbone by PCR.** Template: pVax1_AG_eGFP. This amplifies
the backbone *outward* from the eGFP site, so the product is the whole vector
minus eGFP, with the UTR rails at its ends.

| Primer | Sequence 5'→3' |
|---|---|
| Backbone F | `GCTGCCTTCTGCGGGGCTTG` |
| Backbone R | `GGTGGCTCTTATATTTCTTCTTACT` |

Use a high-fidelity polymerase (Q5, Phusion). Expected product = your plasmid
size minus the eGFP ORF — confirm against your own map before you commit.

**2. Destroy the template.** `DpnI`, 1 µL into the finished PCR, 37 °C for
15–30 min. Skipping this is the single most common reason a "cloning" plate
comes back full of parental eGFP colonies. Then gel-purify or column-clean.

**3. Assemble.** NEBuilder HiFi or In-Fusion, per the kit. Aim for a 2:1 to 3:1
molar ratio of fragment to vector; with a ~0.4–0.6 kb insert and a multi-kb
backbone that is roughly 25–50 ng of fragment to 100 ng of vector. 50 °C,
15–60 min.

Run a **no-insert control** (vector + assembly mix, no fragment) on every
attempt. Its colony count is your background, and without it a successful plate
and a failed one look identical.

**4. Transform.** 2 µL of the assembly into 50 µL competent cells. Ice 30 min →
42 °C for 30–45 s → ice 2 min → add 950 µL SOC → 37 °C, 1 h, shaking. Plate on
**kanamycin** (pVAX1-family vectors are Kan-R, not Amp — plating on ampicillin
will give you a clean lawn of nothing).

**5. Screen.** Colony PCR with Backbone R (or your own 5'-UTR primer) paired
with a reverse primer inside the insert. Pick 4–8 colonies per construct;
these assemblies are usually efficient enough that 4 is plenty.

**6. Sequence before you trust anything.** Sanger across both junctions, or
whole-plasmid nanopore, which now costs about the same and catches backbone
rearrangements that two Sanger reads miss. Check specifically:

- the `GCCACC`**`ATG`** junction is intact and in frame,
- the ORF has no indels (a single-base slip silently truncates everything),
- **P7/P8: the P2A element is intact** — it is the only thing making these two
  constructs bicistronic,
- **P6/P7/P8: the protein ends in `GYQTI` with nothing after it.** This is
  positional. Anything appended abolishes AP-3 binding and the construct
  silently reroutes to the plasma membrane, which looks like a negative result
  rather than a cloning error.

**7. Miniprep / midiprep, then IVT** with your existing primers. The poly(A)
tail is added by PCR, as in your current workflow — see the note below.

## The poly(A) decision

These fragments **do not carry an encoded poly(A) tract**, because your IVT
reverse primer adds the tail by PCR. Two reasons, and the second is the one
that mattered:

1. If the primer adds it, an encoded tract does no work for the transcript.
2. A 120-nt A-tract is close to unsynthesisable. Ordering the full cassette
   with it encoded flags immediately: *"121-nt A-tract: most vendors cannot
   synthesise this reliably"*, plus 81 repeated 40-mers. Vendors will fail,
   refuse, or quietly deliver a contracted tract — and it contracts again in
   *E. coli*.

The tail your transcript gets therefore comes entirely from the PCR step. That
is the same as your current workflow, but it is now the *only* source, so it is
worth confirming once: run the IVT product against an untailed control on a
denaturing gel or TapeStation, or do an RNase H + oligo-dT assay. A tail that
was never added looks exactly like one that was.

## What these fragments do *not* change

`--mode orf` keeps your backbone's existing 5' and 3' UTRs. The validated
BNT162b2 UTRs now in the toolkit (`UTR5_hAg`, `UTR3_AES_mtRNR1`) are **not** in
these fragments. That is deliberate for a first build: your backbone is known to
work and the IVT primers are unchanged.

To upgrade the UTRs later, `txv order --mode cassette` emits fragments carrying
them — but that moves the primer-annealing rails, so the IVT primers must be
re-designed. Run it as its own arm (same ORF, old UTR vs new UTR) rather than
changing two things at once.

## Verifying the assembly in silico

Benchling will simulate the Gibson/HiFi assembly directly, which is the better
check since the backbone lives there. If you would rather have it here, drop
`pvax1_ag_egfp.gb` into the repo and:

```python
from txv.order import assemble_into_backbone, synthesis_fragment
from txv.pvax1_ag import build_panel_construct

construct = build_panel_construct("P3")
plasmid = assemble_into_backbone(backbone_sequence, construct.orf, "pVax1_AG_P3")
```

It refuses rather than guessing if either rail is not unique in the backbone.
